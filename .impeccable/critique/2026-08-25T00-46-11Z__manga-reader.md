## Design Specificity Verdict

The app feels authored for manga rather than category-interchangeable. Ink & Seal creates a coherent language through paper/ink surfaces, vermilion marks, serif display type, monospaced metadata, screentone placeholders, and cover-led composition. The missed opportunity is product specificity: the app looks like a distinctive manga reader, but its information architecture does not yet lead with its public product promise of helping readers keep current with favorite manga.

The deterministic detector returned zero findings, but it is markup-oriented and its native clean result is not evidence of SwiftUI quality. Native source review found systemic fixed-size typography, contrast, touch-target, motion-preference, and adaptivity concerns that the detector cannot see. Browser overlays are inapplicable to a native SwiftUI target.

## Overall Impression

The visual identity and core reading loop are already unusually coherent. The biggest opportunity is to turn returning for new favorite chapters into the emotional and structural center of the app rather than making users infer it from small Library badges and a hidden refresh gesture.

## What's Working

1. Ink & Seal is a recognizable system, not scattered decoration.
2. The detail-to-reader flow is state-aware: Start, Continue, next chapter, and reread reduce decisions at the moment of action.
3. Destructive history and MyAnimeList account states explain what is preserved or removed.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The “keep me current” promise is buried

Returning readers see provider-wide feeds first. Personal updates appear only as numeric Library badges and require an undisclosed pull-to-refresh. Create a first-class saved-title Updates surface, visible unread totals, freshness status, and a direct refresh affordance. Suggested command: `$impeccable shape`.

### [P1] Dynamic Type and compact-width resilience are structurally weak

Fixed 10–12 pt brand fonts, fixed rail covers, a permanent three-column Library grid, and horizontal detail composition will not scale cleanly. Make branded fonts relative to text styles, scale dependent metrics, and branch grids/heroes for accessibility sizes and width. Suggested command: `$impeccable adapt`.

### [P1] Important gestures and secondary actions are hidden

Pull-to-refresh, long-press chapter actions, and tap-to-reveal reader chrome require recall. Add visible or first-run cues and accessible alternatives while keeping gestures as shortcuts. Suggested command: `$impeccable onboard`.

### [P2] Identical source controls have inconsistent scope

Home’s source chip changes the global browse source; Search’s visually identical chip changes only local search scope. Label the local scope explicitly, use a filter control, or unify behavior. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`.

### [P2] Recovery and empty-state next steps are inconsistent

Search has Retry, while Home errors are passive and empty Library/collection states describe actions without offering them. Add contextual actions that take users directly to recovery or the next task. Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`.

## Persona Red Flags

**Maya, returning weekly reader:** Cannot answer “what changed in my favorites?” from Home; must decode numeric Library badges and remember a hidden refresh gesture.

**Jordan, first-time reader:** Empty Library offers no route to browse, personalization silently remains absent during cold start, and chapter/reader gestures are undiscoverable.

**Alex, accessibility user:** Fixed brand fonts, three-column grids, 40 pt reader controls, and unconditioned spatial animation undermine large text, motor access, and Reduce Motion.

## Minor Observations

- Confirm bottom content remains reachable above the tab bar across OS versions.
- “Read” is brandful but can sound like an action instead of a destination.
- Numeric unread badges need a clearer accessible and visible meaning.
- Settings visually groups source selection and adult-content permission even though they are distinct decisions.

## Questions to Consider

- If the promise is “never miss a favorite chapter,” why is provider popularity ahead of saved-title updates?
- Is Home primarily a catalog, a personal reading dashboard, or a deliberate blend?
- What should make opening the app after a week feel rewarding before the first tap?
- Is Source a durable user-facing concept, or infrastructure the app should increasingly hide?
